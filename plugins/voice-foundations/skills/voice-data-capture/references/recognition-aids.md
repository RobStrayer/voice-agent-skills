## What aids can and cannot do

- **They bias. They do not verify.** A keyterm list, phrase list or prompt raises the odds that a listed term appears. Google's adaptation page says a higher boost can also raise false positives, meaning a phrase shows up in the transcript that was not said ([Google](https://cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/v2/docs/adaptation-model)). AssemblyAI says large lists or common terms can cause overcorrections and hallucinations, and recommends starting with none ([AssemblyAI](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/streaming/prompting-and-keyterms.md)). The vendors describe the risk differently, so test on your own audio.
- **They suit open vocabulary you know in advance.** Brand names, product and service names, staff names, local street names, your domain list. *Inference:* keyterm and phrase lists cannot list arbitrary digits or letters, so they do not help with them. Google's class tokens are the exception: its adaptation page says a class stands for a common concept, and its example is a street number that should be recognized whatever the number is ([Google](https://cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/v2/docs/adaptation-model)). The list of tokens includes digit and alphanumeric sequences (see the Google row below). For the rest, use the stage settings below and the [protocols](entity-protocols.md).
- **Do not prime the recognizer with the answer you are about to check.** *Inference:* vendor pages suggest passing known names and IDs as hints so they are spelled correctly. AssemblyAI's "detailed context" level lists participant names and account identifiers, Deepgram's Flux page suggests adding a customer's name to keyterms right before you ask for it, and OpenAI's example passes an account ID as a keyword. That helps transcription. It also weakens any check that compares the transcript with the value you primed, because the transcript leans toward the expected value. For identity checks, collect first and compare in code.
- **Hints are data you send to a vendor.** Amazon Transcribe says not to enter confidential information, personal information (PII) or protected health information (PHI) into a custom vocabulary ([Amazon Transcribe](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/transcribe/latest/dg/custom-vocabulary.html)). *Inference:* treat every vendor's hint lists and prompts the same way unless the contract and data settings say otherwise ([security guide](../../voice-agent-security/references/security-guide.md#sensitive-data-in-every-store)).
- **Some providers ignore a bad aid without an error.** Deepgram treats `term:0.15` as one literal term, so it silently boosts nothing ([Deepgram](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/keyterm)). Google ignores an invalid or malformed class token without an error ([Google](https://cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/v2/docs/adaptation-model)). AssemblyAI ignores keyterms over 50 characters ([AssemblyAI](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/streaming/prompting-and-keyterms.md)). Speechmatics drops a custom dictionary element longer than 6 words before transcription starts ([Speechmatics](https://docs.speechmatics.com/speech-to-text/features/custom-dictionary)). Test that an aid is active: transcribe a fixture with and without it and compare ([test fixtures](test-fixtures.md)).

## Change settings for the field you are collecting

Speech changes from field to field: names, digits, yes or no. Several providers let you change recognition settings during a call, so set them for the question you are about to ask and restore them afterwards.

- **Deepgram Flux.** A `Configure` message can replace `keyterms` (the whole list, up to 100 terms). It can turn `numerals` on, so digits come back as digits. It can also raise `eot_threshold` and `eot_timeout_ms`, so a long answer is not cut off. The Flux page describes these uses for long answers such as an OTP or an account number ([Flux Configure](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/flux/configure.md)). The 2026-08-28 changelog gives an example for an 8-character alphanumeric account ID. In it you raise `eot_threshold` to `1.0`, which suppresses natural end of turn, collect all 8 characters, end the turn yourself with `ForceEndTurn`, and then return to the default ([changelog](https://developers.deepgram.com/changelog/2026/8/28)). `eot_timeout_ms` (500 to 60000) is a safety net that still ends the turn.
- **AssemblyAI streaming.** An `UpdateConfiguration` message can replace `keyterms_prompt` and change `min_turn_silence` and `max_turn_silence` on Universal-3 Pro and Universal Streaming. It can change `prompt` on Universal-3 Pro only. The page says higher `min_turn_silence` reduces entity splitting on numbers and proper nouns, and that `max_turn_silence` should go up when a caller reads out a number or address ([updating configuration](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/streaming/updating-configuration-mid-stream.md)). The voice agent best practices page shows an email split into "It's John.", "Smith." and "At gmail.com." with `min_turn_silence` 100 and `max_turn_silence` 1000 ms, and kept as one turn at 400 and 2000 ms. It also lists a "Patient" preset of 200 and 2000 ms for entity dictation ([best practices](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/voice-agents/best-practices.md)).
- **OpenAI `gpt-live-transcribe`.** Another `session.update` can change `prompt`, `keywords`, `languages` and `delay` during a session ([realtime transcription](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime-transcription)).
- **Cartesia Ink.** Keyterms are set only when the connection opens and cannot change mid-stream ([Cartesia](https://docs.cartesia.ai/use-the-api/stt/keyterms.md)).

*Inference:* a longer wait for the end of a turn means more silence after the caller finishes. Keep the long setting for the one field, and end the turn yourself as soon as you know the entry is complete: the digit count is reached, the caller pressed `#`, or a check symbol validates.

One vendor example points the wrong way for card data. AssemblyAI's best practices page shows a "payment stage" that raises `max_turn_silence` to 3000 ms "for credit card dictation". Do not copy that for card numbers. Card digits should not pass through the recognizer and the model at all ([privacy](../SKILL.md#keep-sensitive-data-off-the-model-path)).

## Number formatting switches

- **Deepgram `numerals`** (default `false`) turns spoken numbers into digits, such as "nine hundred" into 900, for listed languages. On Flux, set it with the query parameter and change it mid-stream with `Configure` ([numerals](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/numerals.md)). The Flux page suggests turning it on right before a PIN, phone number or order number.
- **Deepgram `smart_format`** formats dates, times, currency, phone numbers, emails and URLs in English. In streaming, an entity that looks incomplete waits for more speech or for 3 seconds of silence before it is finalized. `no_delay=true` finalizes at once and skips formatting in many cases ([smart format](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/smart-format.md)).
- **Twilio ConversationRelay `deepgramSmartFormat`** is listed with a default of `true` when Deepgram is the transcription provider, and it reformats dates, times, currency, numbers and addresses ([ConversationRelay](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml/connect/conversationrelay)). If you use that path, check what a digit answer looks like before you write the support check.
- *Inference:* formatted output is a guess with nicer punctuation. Parse the formatted text in code, and do not treat a well-formed "415-555-0123" or "jon.smith@gmail.com" as validated.
- *Inference:* these switches also change what the [support check](validate-and-store.md#check-that-the-transcript-supports-the-value) can see. That check fails when the answer holds a number word such as "fifteen" or "fifty", which tells you a field that should be digit by digit needs asking again. If the recognizer has already turned them into digits, the signal may be gone, and "fifteen" can look like "one five". For a digit-by-digit field, either keep formatting off for the transcript you check, or keep it on and treat a multi-digit group, or a digit count other than the one you asked for, as a reason to ask again. Store which setting was on.

## Record what was on

*Inference:* store, with each captured value, the recognizer and model, the aids and their list version, `numerals` and turn settings, and whether any were changed for that field. When a value is wrong you can then tell a recognition problem from a settings problem ([validate and store](validate-and-store.md#what-to-store-with-each-value)).
